\section{Reinforcement Learning Refinement (GRPO)}
\label{sec:grpo}

We refine the SFT adapter from \S\ref{sec:sft} with Group Relative
Policy Optimization (GRPO) using a deterministic rule-based reward;
the reward is byte-aligned with the chain-accuracy eval metric, so no
separate reward model is trained.

\paragraph{Rollouts.}
For each training prompt $p$ with ground-truth chain
$\mathbf{c}^*\!=\!(c^*_1,\dots,c^*_K)$, the trainable policy
$\pi_\theta$ samples $G\!=\!4$ completions $\{\mathbf{o}_g\}_{g=1}^{G}$
at $T\!=\!0.9$, top-$k\!=\!0$, top-$p\!=\!1.0$, min-$p\!=\!0$ (TRL
\texttt{GRPOTrainer} defaults). An earlier restrictive configuration
($T\!=\!0.7$, top-$k\!=\!20$, top-$p\!=\!0.9$, min-$p\!=\!0.05$)
collapsed the $G$ rollouts onto identical completions for our
deterministic SFT base, eliminating intra-group reward variance and
zeroing the policy gradient. Each $\mathbf{o}_g$ is parsed into a
chain $\hat{\mathbf{c}}_g$.

\paragraph{Reward.}
The reward uses a format gate followed by a continuous partial-credit
score with a perfect-match bonus:
\begin{equation}
R(\mathbf{o}_g,\mathbf{c}^*)
\;=\;
\begin{cases}
0 & \text{if format invalid} \\
\tfrac{1}{2}\,\rho_g \;+\; \tfrac{1}{2}\,\mathbb{1}[\text{perfect}] & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
\;\in\;[0,\,1]
\end{equation}
where the \emph{format gate} fires (returning $0$) on parse failure,
unknown agent IDs, or duplicate IDs; the \emph{position-accuracy} term
\begin{equation}
\rho_g \;=\; \frac{n_\text{correct}(\hat{\mathbf{c}}_g,\mathbf{c}^*)}
                 {\max(|\hat{\mathbf{c}}_g|,\,K)}
\;\in\;[0,\,1]
\end{equation}
counts set-aware position matches normalised by the longer chain
(implicit length penalty); and $\mathbb{1}[\text{perfect}]\!=\!1$ iff
$|\hat{\mathbf{c}}_g|\!=\!K$ and $\rho_g\!=\!1$. The set-aware
matching $\equiv_\text{set}$ at each position permits agents declared
interchangeable (e.g.\ $\{$Music, Ambience$\}$). Empirically the SFT
base produces format errors at rate $0$ ($212/212$ rollouts in our
smoke runs all passed format checks), so the gate is a
GRPO-exploration safeguard against drift rather than an active term;
the $50/50$ split between $\rho_g$ and the perfect bonus gives dense
partial-credit gradient on near-misses while making a perfect output
$2.5\!\times$ the reward of a $4/5$-correct partial, preventing
satisficing on near-perfect chains.

\paragraph{Advantage and loss.}
Within each group of $G$ rollouts on the same prompt,
\begin{equation}
\hat{A}_g \;=\; \frac{R_g-\mu}{\sigma+\varepsilon},\quad
\mu=\tfrac{1}{G}\!\sum_g R_g,\;
\sigma=\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{G}\!\sum_g (R_g-\mu)^2}.
\end{equation}
Groups with $\sigma\!<\!\varepsilon\!=\!10^{-6}$ contribute zero
advantage by construction; under data-parallel training these groups
are not skipped at the loop level (which would desynchronise NCCL
collective calls across ranks and deadlock), but rather their loss
contribution is masked to zero via the
$\text{valid\_mask}\!\times\!\text{aligned\_mask}$ product, yielding a
zero gradient and a no-op optimiser step. The single-step on-policy
objective combines an advantage-weighted log-likelihood and a KL
anchor against the frozen SFT snapshot $\pi_\text{ref}$:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}(\theta)
\;=\;
-\,\mathbb{E}_{(g,t)}\!\left[\hat{A}_g\,\log\pi_\theta(c_{g,t}\,|\,p,c_{g,<t})\right]
\;+\;\beta\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\widehat{\text{KL}}_{K3}(\pi_\theta\!\parallel\!\pi_\text{ref})\right]
\end{equation}
with $\widehat{\text{KL}}_{K3}=\hat{r}-\log\hat{r}-1$ and
$\hat{r}=\pi_\text{ref}(c_{g,t})/\pi_\theta(c_{g,t})$ (Schulman's
unbiased non-negative KL estimator). The sequence-level $\hat{A}_g$ is
broadcast uniformly over completion tokens of rollout $g$.

\paragraph{Implementation.}
A custom rollout loop replaces \texttt{model.generate()} so logits are
cast to fp32 before softmax and multinomial — bf16 long-tail underflow
on Qwen's $152$K-vocab softmax otherwise produces NaN probability rows
that crash \texttt{torch.multinomial}.\footnote{This caused a
TRL-based prototype to crash five times within the first $400$ steps
before the custom sampler was written.} The $G$ rollouts of the same
prompt share a single batched forward pass (the $\sim 7$K-token
prompt is encoded once at batch size $G$ rather than $G$ times
sequentially), reducing per-step rollout time from $\approx 19$\,s to
$\approx 12$\,s on Qwen3-8B. A single shared base carries two named
LoRA adapters (\textit{policy}, trainable; \textit{ref}, frozen),
saving $\approx 14$\,GB of GPU memory versus two independent
\texttt{PeftModel} instances. Multi-GPU training uses 4-rank
\texttt{torchrun} DDP with manual gradient all-reduce: the standard
\texttt{DistributedDataParallel} wrapper conflicts with PEFT's
\texttt{set\_adapter} forward hooks, so each rank holds an independent
copy of the base + adapters, processes its own dataset shard
($\text{idx\_order}[\text{rank}::\text{world\_size}]$ with the same
shuffle seed across ranks for non-overlapping coverage), and after
backward each trainable parameter's gradient is averaged across ranks
via \texttt{dist.all\_reduce}.

\paragraph{Configuration.}
$G\!=\!4$, $\beta\!=\!0.04$, lr $1\!\times\!10^{-6}$, bf16 with
gradient checkpointing, gradient norm clipped to $1$, $3$ epochs over
$1396$ training prompts. Only the policy LoRA's $\sim 87$M parameters
are trainable. Single 4-GPU H200 DDP run, yielding
$1396\!\times\!3 / 4 \approx 1047$ optimiser steps and
$\approx 5$--$6$\,hours wall clock for the full budget.
